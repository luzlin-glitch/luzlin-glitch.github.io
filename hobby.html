From K-pop to Nepal

How Exploring Different Cultures Became My Favorite Hobby

Dance, photography, anime, Japanese learning, and international volunteering are all connected by cultural curiosity.

Cultural Exploration in Nepal

This photo represents one of my most memorable cultural experiences. Volunteering in Nepal helped me connect my interests in culture, communication, and global learning.

Featured Article

My hobbies began with entertainment, but they gradually became a way for me to understand different cultures. Among all my interests, K-pop has influenced me the most. At first, I was simply attracted by the music, choreography, and stage design. However, after I started learning dance more seriously, I realized that K-pop is not only about performance. It also reflects teamwork, discipline, visual storytelling, and the way popular culture travels across languages and borders.

Dance later brought me into photography and video editing. In the K-pop Dancing Club at NTHU, I helped film and edit promotional materials for many teams. Standing behind the camera changed the way I watched performances. I learned to notice movement, facial expression, lighting, and the relationship between the performer and the audience. Through this process, photography became more than recording memories. It became a tool for interpreting people, emotions, and moments.

My interest in culture also extends to Japanese anime. Anime first attracted me through its stories and character designs, but it later encouraged me to learn Japanese. As I study the language, I begin to understand cultural details that subtitles cannot fully express, such as politeness, humor, and emotional distance between characters. Learning Japanese makes me feel that culture is not something far away. It can be entered slowly through language, stories, and daily practice.

Another important cultural experience for me was volunteering in Nepal. During my stay, I lived in a very different environment and worked with people from different countries. Nepal taught me that cultural exploration is not always romantic or easy. Sometimes it means adjusting to unfamiliar routines, communicating with limited resources, and learning to respect different ways of living. Still, those challenges made the experience unforgettable because they pushed me to become more open-minded and flexible.

These hobbies have also influenced the way I create content. When I design a post, edit a video, or take a photo, I often ask myself what kind of cultural message is being delivered. A dance cover can show admiration for another culture, a photo can preserve the atmosphere of a place, and a short video can introduce an experience to people who have never been there. Because of this, I no longer see my hobbies as only relaxing activities. They are also small ways to communicate with others.

Looking back, dance, photography, K-pop, anime, Japanese learning, and volunteering in Nepal may seem like separate hobbies. To me, they are all connected by one idea: the desire to explore cultures through experience. These interests allow me to see the world from different angles. They also remind me that learning does not only happen in classrooms. It can happen on stage, behind a camera, through a song, in a foreign language, or in a village far away from home. That is why exploring cultures has become not only my hobby, but also a part of who I am. Through them, I keep practicing curiosity, empathy, and courage in my everyday life :)
