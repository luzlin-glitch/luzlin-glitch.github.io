Writing Guide

Let's Dig In

Understanding the four key elements of expository writing.

1. Thesis Statement

A thesis statement clearly presents the central idea of an essay.

Good Example

Dogs have become family-like companions in South Korea because loneliness, low birth rates, and single-person households have changed how people seek emotional connection.

Why it works: The sentence identifies the topic, explains the cause, and previews the article's direction.

Weak Example

Many people in South Korea like dogs.

Why it doesn't work: The idea is too general and does not explain the article's main argument.

2. Cohesive Writing

Cohesion connects sentences through repeated ideas and logical relationships.

Good Example

South Korea has more single-person households. This loneliness helps explain why more people turn to dogs for companionship.

Why it works: The phrase this loneliness links the two sentences smoothly.

Weak Example

South Korea has more single-person households. Pet jackets can be expensive.

Why it doesn't work: Readers cannot easily see the relationship between the two ideas.

3. Coherent Writing

Coherence refers to the logical organization of paragraphs.

Dog Funeral

↓

Loneliness & Demographic Change

↓

Dogs as Family Members

↓

Pet Industry Growth

↓

Changing Social Values

↓

Return to Pet Funeral

This structure helps readers move naturally from a personal story to a broader social explanation.

4. Takeaway

Strong Takeaway

In a society where many people feel isolated, dogs have become a new form of family and emotional companionship.

This conclusion connects the title, the examples, and the article's broader social message.
